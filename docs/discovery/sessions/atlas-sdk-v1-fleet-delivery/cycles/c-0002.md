## Questions and Answers

### Q1 (grounded) - What does the deterministic gate check on a prose surface?

Recommended splitting the rule by surface: full binding check on TypeScript,
narrower avoided-term plus required-term-presence on prose, with distinct
Finding codes. Alternative offered: leave prose out entirely and scope #117 to
`scripts/**/*.ts`.

Answered indirectly, and the answer **revised the question rather than picking
an option**: every Atlas has its own rules split into deterministic and
non-deterministic; deterministic always runs first; then an agent holding the
whole Atlas in context runs the Atlas's rules and laws and shakes out
inconsistencies. Prose consistency is therefore not a deterministic concern at
all, so neither offered option was on the right axis.

**Two of the three components were already canonical**, so this cycle treated
them as confirmation rather than decision: `CONTEXT.md` already specifies the
ordering under Lint, already names Atlas-owned deterministic checks as the
**Check SDK**, already defines **Atlas Policy** as declaring deterministic or
semantic evaluation, and already defines Dispute, Contradiction, and Divergence
for inconsistency shake-out.

### Q2 (follow-up) - Is the context-window bound an invariant or an implementation detail?

The third component was **not** canonical. A negative search of `CONTEXT.md` for
context window, size, fit, and token language returned no match; `Atlas` is
bounded to a *directory*, not to a context window.

Recommended recording it as a hard invariant in an Architecture Decision Record.
Alternative offered: a Manifest guideline with warning severity and chunked
degradation.

Answer: "Record as a guideline. It's one of the underlying principles of why
we're splitting big wikis into smaller chunks. Easier to do non-deterministic
checks and ensure quality." **Settled - alternative accepted**, with a rationale
broader than the alternative as offered.

### Q3 (follow-up) - Does lane zero ship with a knowingly unprotected surface?

Recommended shipping it, closing #117 explicitly narrowed, and re-filing the
prose half. Alternative offered: hold lane zero until an avoided-terms-only
closed-set scan covers prose too.

Answer: "Ship it, close 117 -> and then let's open up whatever is necessary to
fill in the gap. It's better to ship issues and keep them small than it is to
hold them open for long periods of time." **Settled - recommendation accepted,
plus a general delivery principle stated unprompted.**
